# Mobile-base IK (branch `mobile-base`, from 2026-11-25)

**Question.** On native starts, learned + AL-SVGD solves 480/480 on every record row. Does putting
every arm on a holonomic planar base make the benchmark harder, so that success leaves the ceiling
and the four method combinations separate? This answers the CLAUDE.md open item "a harder problem
formulation" (and the 7/21 notes' mobile-base IK).

**Prior, from `../codebase`: a base may make it EASIER.** On its Basic Mobile Manipulator (KMR iiwa) row,
the old formulation's IPOPT success went from 46.9% on the fixed-base Arm on a Table to 68.4%
(new formulation 69.3 to 85.4). So the pilot measures difficulty before anything scales, and
reports it if the problem did not get harder.

## The formulation (approved by Thomas, 2026-11-25)

**Base.**
- `b = (x, y, yaw)`: prismatic x, prismatic y and revolute yaw from the world to the platform. This is
  the KMR URDF's structure, in `models/mobile_base/kmr_platform.urdf`.
- Bounds are the plant's own limits: x and y in [-2, 2] m, yaw in [-2 pi, 2 pi]. They are a variable
  bound in both arms, because every start is drawn inside them (yaw in [-pi, pi]).
- The arm base frame `A` is welded to the platform's mount frame. `X_WA(b) = X_WF(b) X_FA`, with
  `X_FA` read from the plant and the planar map checked against the plant's joint chain.

**Learned arm.**
- The variables are `[b | c | z | q_c]`.
- `c` keeps its fixed-base meaning: the WORLD pose of the flow frame. Its box (+-0.25 grasp,
  +-1 pose), its native start (`GraspCStart`, target flow pose), the latent box and trust region,
  and the correction with its weight 10 are all unchanged.
- The network is handed `X_WA(b)^-1 X(c)`, which is `c` in the arm frame, the frame jrl's FK and the
  training data use: `VarsToQ = [b ; flow(X_WA(b)^-1 X(c), z) + q_c]`.
- Implementation: `num_task_vars = 9` and the existing `TaskVarsToPose7` hook. The AutoDiffXd chain
  rule is the existing one.
- **Fibre structure:** where the chart is exact, the end effector's world pose is `X(c)` whatever
  `b` and `z` are, so the base joins the latent as a coordinate along the IK fibre.
- **Measured risk, not fixed:** a far base start conditions the flow off its training workspace.
  `start_conditioning_reach` per cell records the distance; it is 1.7 m on a uniform start in the
  first smoke, against a Panda reach of about 0.85 m.

**Joint space.** The variables are `[b | q_arm]`; `VarsToQ` is the identity into the plant slots.

**Shared.**
- The configuration (`Config`, the joint-limit row, centering) is the arm only. The base block of
  the centering cost is zero, as in `../codebase`: a base has no home.
- Collision is exact on the full plant, platform included.

**Calibration.** `CalibrateFlowFrame` measures the flow frame against `ArmBaseFrame()` (`A`) on a
mobile program, at random bases. The spread then also proves base invariance. Fixed-base programs
take the old world-frame path, unchanged.

**AL-SVGD kernels.**
- Learned: `bcz` (b, c and z factors, each its own median bandwidth).
- Joint space: `bq` (a base factor times the arm-`q` factor).
- Base-blind kernels are refused on a mobile program.
- The swarm's configuration is `[q_arm | b]`, arm first.

## The scene (Thomas chose the KMR-style layout, 2026-11-25)

This is `../codebase`'s Basic Mobile Manipulator room, with the numbers copied and nothing imported:
- **Shelves:** four shelf units at (+-0.85, 0) and (0, +-0.85), centre z 1.3967, facing the centre.
  They stand on a 2.1 x 2.2 m central table, whose top is at z 0.9967.
- **Platform:** the KMP200 collision box, 1.1216 x 0.6304 x 0.6672 m, with the arm mounted at
  (0.363, -0.184, 0.7) and yaw -90 deg.
- **Shelf unit:** this repo's `models/assets/shelves.sdf`. The compartment definition is therefore
  the hardened scene's, and only the welds differ (`MOBILE_SHELF_WELDS`).
- **Arm blocks:** each robot's arm block (arm, gripper, arm-internal filters) is lifted textually
  from its hardened scene. Only the base weld moves.
- **Filter:** one filter group, the arm's base link against the platform. On the soft PCS arm it
  also covers the first sub-link spheres, the ones its hardened scene filters against the tables.
- **Generation:** generated by `src/mobile_base/generate_scenes.py`, with byte-equality tested.
  The new files give a new `scene_fingerprint`, so these runs never pair with the record.

**Targets.**
- `(b, q_arm)` is drawn uniformly, then required to be collision-free and to have its fingertip in a
  compartment of the mobile room (mug screen on grasp).
- Acceptance is about 1 in 9000 draws on all four robots (smoke, 2026-11-25), so the rejection cap
  is 2e6 under `--scene mobile` (`../codebase`'s PR2 used the same).

## Base-start rules (Thomas: "try all three")

There is one `q_init` per cell, drawn from the cell's own stream (`CellRng`). Both arms read it:
joint space starts at all of it, the learned arm at its base.

| rule | base start |
| --- | --- |
| `uniform` | x, y ~ U(-2, 2), yaw ~ U(-pi, pi), independent of the target |
| `facing` | `../codebase`'s `facing_axis` + jitter: stand off 1.0 m x U(0.6, 1.4) behind the target along its approach axis, heading +-20 deg |
| `home` | (1.5, 0, 0) every cell (the KMR directive's default) |

The facing rule's approach axis is the gripper's wrist-to-fingertip direction in the target frame,
the same physical axis on every robot. On the grasp task it reads the generating grasp's approach
direction, which is the information `GraspCStart` already uses.

## Robots

All four are on the base: Panda, iiwa (n4 on the cluster, n6 locally), screw7_p050 and soft12.
- **Rigid arms:** a mixin (`src/mobile_base/program.py`) wraps the record classes. The arm block is
  moved into its real plant slots and `b` is put in front. Every slot is read from the plant.
- **Soft PCS arm:** its floating sub-link poses are composed with `X_WA(b)` inside the forward-mode
  strain map (`src/mobile_base/soft.py`), which has 15 inputs instead of 12. Grid vectors are
  `[b | strain]`.

Drivers: `--scene mobile --base-start {uniform,facing,home}` on all four benchmark scripts
(`src/mobile_base/harness.py`). Only the learned and numerical arms are allowed.

## Compute (learned-ik-orchestrator, 2026-11-25)

The runs are off volta. The pilot and the scale-up run on Engaging / CSAIL preemptible GPUs, with
ONE GPU type pinned with `--constraint` and the CPU model pinned on Engaging, for every arm, solver
and rule. Seconds never sit beside the V100 record. Ask the orchestrator before each submission
(prefix `mb_`).

## Status (2026-11-25)

- **Built.** All four robots, both arms, all three base-start rules, IPOPT and AL-SVGD.
  - Local CPU smoke tests ran under IPOPT on every robot.
  - AL-SVGD ran on Panda and soft PCS; iiwa and screw are covered at the test level.
- **Tests.**
  - `tests/test_mobile_base.py` covers files, regions, slots, calibration, gradients and the start
    rules.
  - `tests/test_mobile_svgd.py` replays the batched SVGD rows, gradients and kernels against Drake on
    10 programs, to 1e-13. Its slow tier checks the compiled step against the eager one.
  - The whole fast tier passes.
- **One AL-SVGD kernel per run.** `svgd_kernel` is one option, so a mobile run passes `bcz` for the
  learned arm and `bq` for joint space in SEPARATE runs (`--arms learned` / `--arms numerical`) on
  the same grid.
- **Facing starts.** A facing stand-off can admit no collision-free start, for a target over the
  central table. The last draw is then returned and flagged in `metadata["starts_in_collision"]`
  (`../codebase`, BG2: 23% of facing starts in collision, and the filter made no difference).
- **Learned-arm risk, seen in the smoke tests.** Uniform and home bases put the target outside the
  chart's training distribution in the arm frame. Over 20-40 eager steps, unresampled particles run
  away (|q| up to 1e30). The solver's resampling clears them every outer check, and facing starts
  converge.
- **Pilot ready, not run (2026-11-26).**
  - 8 rows x {learned, joint} x {AL-SVGD, IPOPT} x 3 rules x 64 cells, on `rtx3090`.
  - Manifests and ops are in `cluster/MOBILE_BASE_RUNBOOK.md`.
  - It waits on Thomas's go, its placement, and the iiwa `n4` chart (SuperCloud only).
- **The `clearances` backend covers the platform**: equal to the autodiff constraint, values exact
  and gradients to 3e-13, on all four robots and both tasks (pinned in `tests/test_mobile_svgd.py`).
